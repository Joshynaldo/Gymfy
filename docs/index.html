Built for the two minutes between sets

Everything here exists because it was needed standing in front of a rack, one-handed, with the bar already loaded.

Logging that keeps up

Scroll wheels instead of a keyboard — 82.5 kg is two flicks, not four keystrokes. Warm-ups are marked as warm-ups and kept out of your records.

Progressive overload

Your next weight is suggested from what you actually lifted, not a formula on a poster. Hit your target reps and it goes up; miss and it holds.

Timed exercises

Planks, dead hangs, loaded carries. Logged in minutes and seconds, charted by duration, with records of their own — a plank's best is a time, not a weight.

Plate calculator

What to load, per side, from the plates your gym actually owns. Bar weight is set per exercise — the leg press does not have a 20 kg bar on it.

Muscle map

Which muscles you actually trained this week, shaded by volume — and which ones you have been quietly avoiding.

Bring your history

Coming from Hevy, Strong or StrengthLog? Export a CSV there, import it here. Unknown exercises get created rather than dropped, and importing twice adds nothing twice.

Rest timer that finds you

A countdown ring on the workout screen, a +30s button sized for a hand you are not looking at, and a notification for when the phone is already back in your pocket.

Notes per exercise

The bench setting, the grip width, what your lower back said last time. Attached to the exercise, waiting for you next time.

A real export

CSV or JSON, any time, no teaser version. Warm-ups included and labelled, weights in a column that says which unit it is in.

It suggests the next weight, and tells you why

Most trackers are a spreadsheet you fill in. Gymfy reads your last session for an exercise and puts a number in the field before you touch it.

Hit every target rep and the weight steps up. Fall short and it holds you there — because the app that pushes you up regardless is the one you stop trusting.

The rules are yours: increment, rep target, how many sessions at a weight before it moves.

See the week you actually trained

Per-exercise charts for top set, volume and estimated 1RM, with personal records dated so you know when you set them.

And a body map shaded by the volume each muscle took — the fastest way to notice that your rear delts have not been touched since March.

Plus a bodyweight-relative strength rank, so the comparison is to people your size rather than to a number on the internet.

Seven themes, one of them glass

AMOLED black that actually turns the pixels off. High contrast for gym lighting. And Hyper — translucent panes over a slowly drifting colour field, which is what this page is wearing.

Pick any accent colour. Every animation respects your system reduce-motion setting, and respecting it means stopping, not going faster.

Nothing to disclose, because nothing is collected

Gymfy contains no networking code. Not a disabled analytics SDK, not an opt-out — there is nothing in the app that can open a connection. The claim is checked by a test in the repository that fails if anyone adds one.

  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting
  • No advertising and no ad identifiers
  • No account, no sign-in, no identifier created for you
  • Progress photos are copied into the app's private storage and never uploaded
  • Uninstalling deletes everything — there is no copy anywhere else

The flip side, said plainly: because nothing is on a server, there is no backup. Use Export before you change phones.
