Step 1 — weight

An interactive preview with sample data, not a screenshot. Tap the tabs, start the workout and log a set.

01 · Home

Today's workout, one tap away

Open Gymfy and today's day from your split is already waiting: the exercises, the targets and a Start button sized for a thumb.

Your last workout and this week's volume sit right underneath, so you know where you stand before the first set.

02 · Logging

Logging that keeps up

A big one-handed keypad instead of the system keyboard, or tap the plates that are on the bar and the total adds itself up. Weight, reps, save.

Same as last time? Repeat the set with one tap.

Mark warm-up, drop and failure sets, and rate effort as RPE or RIR if you like.

03 · Progressive overload

It suggests the next weight, and tells you why

Most trackers are a spreadsheet you fill in. Gymfy reads your last session for an exercise and puts a number in the field before you touch it.

Hit every target rep and the weight steps up. Fall short and it holds you there — because the app that pushes you up regardless is the one you stop trusting.

The rules are yours: increment, rep target, how many sessions at a weight before it moves.

04 · Rest timer

A rest timer that finds you

The countdown sits at the top of the workout, with +30 s sized for a hand you aren't looking at.

Phone back in your pocket? The workout notification and the watch keep counting, and buzz when it's time to lift.

05 · Progress

See the week you actually trained

Volume, workouts and the muscles that took the most sets, over a week, a month or a year. Per-exercise charts for top set, volume and estimated 1RM.

And a body map shaded by the volume each muscle took — the fastest way to notice that your rear delts have not been touched since March.

06 · All-time

Records, ranks and a year of training

Every personal record, dated so you know when you set it. A year of training days at a glance, and the streak to go with it.

A strength rank puts your big lifts against your bodyweight, so the comparison is to people your size rather than to a number on the internet.

07 · Body

Your body, next to your lifting

Bodyweight, chest, waist and arms, each with its own history chart, and the change since last time right beside it.

Progress photos go into the app's private storage, and two of them side by side show what the mirror has not been telling you.

08 · Themes

Eight themes, one of them glass

AMOLED black that actually turns the pixels off. High contrast for gym lighting. Tokyo Night, Dracula, Catppuccin and Gruvbox for the editor crowd. And Hyper — translucent panes over a slowly drifting colour field, which is what this page and the preview are wearing.

Pick any accent colour. Try one: the preview changes with it.

Every animation respects your system reduce-motion setting, and respecting it means stopping, not going faster.

09 · More

Everything else, one tap away

About 1,270 exercises with animated previews, six ready-made programs, the plate and 1RM calculators, sharing a plan with a training partner, and settings in English or German.

No hub of upsells and no locked tiles: everything listed here is in the free app.

Everything else it does

Built for the two minutes between sets: everything here exists because it was needed standing in front of a rack, one-handed, with the bar already loaded.

Programs and supersets

Start from one of six ready-made programs, plan sets at a percentage of your 1RM, and run training blocks with a deload week. Superset neighbours, reorder, or swap and add exercises mid-workout.

Timed exercises

Planks, dead hangs, loaded carries. Logged in minutes and seconds, charted by duration, with records of their own — a plank's best is a time, not a weight.

Muscle map

Which muscles you actually trained this week, shaded by volume. Switch to fatigue to see what is still recovering, and which muscles you have been quietly skipping.

Your month in review

Workouts, volume, time trained, top lifts, records and most-trained muscles, set against the month before, plus a Year in Training for the long view. Share one as an image if you feel like it.

Goals that fill themselves in

A weight on a lift by a date, a number of workouts a week, or a bodyweight to reach. Progress comes from what you log, sits on the Home tab, and gets a small celebration when you get there.

Wear OS companion

Your workout, set count and rest countdown on the watch, with a double buzz when it is time to lift. Log the next set with the suggested weight and reps, nudged with the crown, add 30 seconds or skip the rest — all without touching the phone.

Share your plan

Send a split to a training partner as a .gymfy file or a printable PDF, or import theirs. Only the plan goes in the file, never your sessions, measurements or photos.

Bring your history

Coming from Hevy, Strong or StrengthLog? Export a CSV there and import it here. Gymfy even rebuilds your split from the history and puts each day on the weekday you train it.

Notes per exercise

The bench setting, the grip width, what your lower back said last time. Attached to the exercise, waiting for you next time.

Health Connect, if you want it

Off until you switch it on. Finished workouts go into Health Connect, next to the rest of your fitness data, and weigh-ins saved there fill in your bodyweight. Health Connect lives on the phone too, so none of it needs a network.

Back up, restore, export

One backup file holds everything, photos included, and restores on this phone or a new one. Turn on automatic backups into a folder you pick, or export to CSV or JSON for a spreadsheet.

English and Deutsch

Gymfy follows your phone's language, or pick one in Settings. Every screen, the notifications and the watch app are translated, down to "82,5 kg". Exercise names stay in English, the way most gyms say them.

Nothing to disclose, because nothing is collected

Gymfy contains no networking code. Not a disabled analytics SDK, not an opt-out — there is nothing in the app that can open a connection. The claim is checked by a test in the repository that fails if anyone adds one.

  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting
  • No advertising and no ad identifiers
  • No account, no sign-in, no identifier created for you
  • Progress photos are copied into the app's private storage and never uploaded
  • Health Connect is off until you turn it on. It is Android's own health store on the phone, not a server, and which other apps may read it is your call
  • The watch app gets only the workout that's running. It talks to your phone through Google Play services: directly over Bluetooth, or through Google's servers end-to-end encrypted when Bluetooth isn't available
  • Uninstalling deletes everything — the only copies left are backups or exports you saved yourself, and workouts you chose to write to Health Connect

The flip side, said plainly: because nothing is on a server, nothing backs itself up to the cloud. Make a backup file before you change phones, by hand or automatically into a folder you pick.
